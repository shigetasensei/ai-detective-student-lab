Why do adults worry about you using AI?

Big question: Why do the adults in your life worry about you using AI?

Step 1. Think alone (2 min). Write one reason an adult might worry. Think about what could go wrong for a kid, not only what could go wrong with the AI.

Step 2. Class sort (5 min). Together, tap a worry card and then tap the column it belongs to. Ask the class: which column fits better, and why? Some worries could fit both.

Step 3. Ask yourself (2 min). Which worry is about me, and which is about the world? Then share with your neighbor.

Step 4. Flip it! 🙃 (1 min) Become a superhero detective. Write one reason adults might be wrong to worry, or one way AI could be your superpower for learning.

Google AI Mode: check it

Big idea: An AI Mode answer is a new summary written from many pages. It is not one quote from one source. Our concern: reading a confident summary and copying it without checking which page said what.

What is different?

Sort it together! Tap a statement, then tap the box it belongs to: A quote or An AI Mode answer. Some statements are tricky, so talk it through with your neighbor first.

Google's own help page says to check important information in more than one place and to click the links for AI sources. Google also says AI Mode can break your question into smaller searches (called query fan-out) and combine what it finds.

Check AI Mode in 5 steps

Quick check

1. Why is an AI Mode answer harder to check than a quote?

Because the sentences are newly written, one sentence may come from several pages, and the links can change. You need to check each claim, not just trust the summary.

2. Is it enough that the answer has links?

No. A link shows where to look. You still need to open it, check the date and author, and see whether it really supports the sentence.

3. What does WHO, WHEN, WHERE, WHY mean here?

WHO wrote the linked page? WHEN was it written or updated? WHERE in the page is the claim? WHY was the page made (to inform, sell or persuade)?

Detective game: check or trust?

What is a chatbot? A chatbot is a computer program you can talk or type to. It writes replies that sound like a person. Many chatbots use generative AI, which makes new sentences from patterns it learned, so a chatbot can be wrong even when it sounds sure. Gemini is one example of a chatbot.
Using Gemini on your school account (for this unit only)
  • Purpose: Use Gemini to research our unit questions only. It is not for games, chatting or personal topics.
  • Check first: Your teacher will confirm the school has turned Gemini on for students.
  • Your data: Google says it does not use data from students' chats to train its models. Your teacher will confirm the school's current settings with IT.
  • Safety filters: Gemini has guardrails, but they are not perfect. If a response seems wrong or unsafe, stop and tell your teacher.
  • Never share: Your full name, address, phone number, passwords or photos of other people.
  • Check every answer: Use WHO, WHEN, WHERE, WHY on the source Gemini gives you, and check it in a second place.
